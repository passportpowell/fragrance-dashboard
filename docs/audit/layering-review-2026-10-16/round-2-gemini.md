### Critique of Sonnet’s Rubric

Sonnet correctly flagged prior hallucinations: chemical claims ("pH," "aeration"), unlisted notes ("balsamic" for Laya), and invented spray settling timings are unsupported by the inventory. 

However, Sonnet’s own counter-rubric replaces one set of inventions with another:
1. **The rigid two-note gate fails perfumery logic:** Sheer molecular primers (e.g., Iso E Super) exist to provide a transparent, textured backdrop without duplicating dense notes. Mandating two shared note names excludes legitimate functional pairings by definition, while having two shared notes (like generic vanilla and amber) can easily yield a cloying, muddy clash.
2. **The 90-ceiling and flattening all kept pairs to 90 is artificial:** Assigning every surviving pair exactly 90 is just threshold-fitting in reverse. It erases subjective nuance between a cohesive anchor pairing and an experimental combination.
3. **Model agreement is not validation:** Two LLMs concurring on a pairing remains pure text pattern matching, not sensory proof.
